# Notice of Authorship and Original Contributions

**Fractus-II: One Shape, Folded at Every Depth**
Author: Ridley DiSiena
Archival record: the commit history of this repository and the DOI assigned to each archived release.
Fractus-II continues the author's earlier work, Fractus (DOI 10.5281/zenodo.23457481), which remains archived.

## What belongs to the author

The framework presented in Fractus-II, and the original concepts, framings, terminology, and syntheses within it, are the original intellectual work of Ridley DiSiena. Anyone who uses, discusses, extends, or builds on them is asked to credit the author by name and cite this work.

The original contributions include, but are not limited to:

- The proposal that spacetime is a single structurally self-similar folded geometry, with particles read as knots in it, forces as creases through it, gravity as its collective flexing, and light as a texture of its surface.
- The tide: the substrate's native, undriven restlessness, with confinement read as trapping the tide rather than supplying its windings.
- The framework's separation of closure (protected identity), the return condition, metastable persistence, and discrete depth with continuous size, and the vocabulary of cinch, harness, and weave built on it.
- The windfold: the tide winding in across a dimensional order, the geometry's convergence from one order into a higher one, with the crease as its record.
- The reading of force channels as creases, connected to iterated relativistic transformations and their fractal limit sets.
- The overturn: mass read as a steady hidden turning within one depth beneath the mass-related phase, with the Higgs boson read as a resonance of the resting state.
- One grip at two depths: the proposal that the Higgs vacuum and the quark condensate are one mechanism seen at two depths.
- The turnings and anchors: mass, charge, color, and spin read as distinct turnings a particle experiences rather than properties it holds.
- The native ringing and anchor matching: the contrast between lepton and quark mixing read through how closely the anchoring of partner families matches.
- The unbranched knot: the reading of charge as what branches the shape into matter and antimatter, and of a Majorana neutrino as the one knot where the branches rejoin.
- Time read through accumulated mass-related phase, and the reading of the photon's zero-interval crossing and entanglement's zero-transmission correlation as one property of the shape seen twice.
- The windfold conjecture: nuclear shell structure as the atom's hidden four-dimensional symmetry carried by windfold into a higher-dimensional, symmetry-broken form.
- The three generations as one harmonic at three depths, with the tau as the boundary where the lepton pattern passes into the nuclear regime.

## What does not belong to the author

Fractus-II draws throughout on established physics. Every established result, theorem, measurement, and model it describes belongs to its discoverers and is credited in the manuscript's appendices and sources. No claim is made to any of it. The claim is to the framework that reads those results together, and to the proposals the manuscript itself marks as conjecture.

## How this work was written

The ideas in Fractus-II began as the author's own thought experiments. They were developed in writing through extended dialogue with Claude, an AI model made by Anthropic, used as reviewer, technical translator, and co-editor, and with OpenAI's GPT models through Codex, used for calculations and checks that ruled out, constrained, and shaped candidate readings. Final editing and proofreading drew on both. The conceptual proposals originate with the author. The AI tools helped name, test, and articulate them.
